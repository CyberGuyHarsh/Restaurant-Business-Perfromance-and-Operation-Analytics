## Business Objective

Business teams need more than raw tables to understand what is happening across restaurants, customers, and delivery operations. This dashboard brings key views into one interactive report so users can monitor available performance indicators, compare results, and investigate patterns that may need attention.

The report is intended to support data-informed discussions. The actual findings and business impact should be established by examining the report and validating the underlying data.

## Business Questions

Depending on the fields and measures in the underlying dataset, the dashboard can help investigate questions such as:

- How does performance differ across restaurants?
- What patterns are visible in customer activity?
- Where do delivery or operational results vary?
- Which categories or locations may warrant closer investigation?
- What additional analysis would help explain the differences observed?

A dashboard can reveal patterns, but further analysis may be needed to determine their causes.

## How to Explore the Report

1. Download `Main Dashboard.pbix` from this repository.
2. Open it using [Microsoft Power BI Desktop](https://powerbi.microsoft.com/desktop/).
3. Navigate between the report pages.
4. Use available slicers, filters, and visual interactions to investigate the data.
5. Select a performance difference and trace it through the relevant pages.
6. Validate important numbers and check the data refresh date before drawing conclusions.

The file may rely on an external data source. Refreshing it may require the original source file, credentials, or updated connection settings.

## How I Would Use the Analysis

A practical business analysis workflow for this project is:

1. **Frame the question:** Define the business decision or performance issue.
2. **Validate the data:** Check for missing values, duplicates, inconsistent categories, and unusual records.
3. **Understand the metrics:** Document each KPI's definition, calculation, and business meaning.
4. **Find patterns:** Compare results across relevant restaurants, customer groups, and periods.
5. **Investigate exceptions:** Look for possible explanations rather than assuming correlation means causation.
6. **Recommend an action:** Link a proposed action to evidence and a measurable follow-up KPI.
